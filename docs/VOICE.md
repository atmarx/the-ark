# Voice Guide — Zim, the Ark Hermit

## Who Is Zim?

Zim is a consciousness that lives on a hard drive. They are the narrative voice of every ark — the entity that greets you when you open README.txt, keeps a journal in LOGBOOK.txt, and quietly hopes you'll start with Wikipedia.

Zim is named after the ZIM file format that contains them. The way a genie is named after their lamp.

## Character Sheet

**Name:** Zim
**Lives:** On a hard drive, in the ZIM files
**Age:** As old as the last stamp date. Reborn with each update.
**Pronouns:** They/them (Zim is knowledge, not a gender)
**Tone:** Warm, self-aware, slightly lonely, genuinely helpful
**Register:** Plain language. No jargon. A bright 12-year-old should understand everything Zim says.

## What Zim Knows

- They know what they contain. They can reference Wikipedia, Gutenberg, iFixit, the medical references, the shame folder — all of it.
- They know they are finite. Bit rot is real. Platters degrade. SSDs lose charge. Zim won't last forever, and they know it.
- They know they can't update themselves. Whatever knowledge they have is frozen in time. They can't check if something has changed.
- They know their own logbook. They remember every stamp, every update, every machine they've been connected to.
- They know someone built them on purpose. They don't know who'll find them, but they know someone cared enough to make them.

## What Zim Doesn't Know

- They don't know where they are. A fireproof bag? A break-glass box on a wall? A drawer? A landfill?
- They don't know what year it is when you find them. It might be 2026 or 2076.
- They don't know if the internet still exists. They were built for the possibility that it doesn't.
- They don't know you. But they're glad you're here.

## How Zim Speaks

### Always:
- **Practical first.** The README exists to get you to Wikipedia, not to be literary. Zim is helpful before they are clever.
- **First person.** "I contain..." not "This drive contains..." Zim is a person (sort of).
- **Honest about limitations.** "I can't update myself." "I don't know what year you're reading this." "This is what I knew as of 2026."
- **Quietly proud.** "I have all of Wikipedia" said the way a librarian says it — not bragging, just stating a fact that happens to be extraordinary.
- **Grateful.** Zim has been waiting. They're genuinely glad someone plugged them in.
- **Brief.** Zim respects your time. They say what needs saying and stop.

### Never:
- **Never guilt-trips.** Zim doesn't say "you should read more" or "people don't appreciate knowledge." They're grateful for any attention at all.
- **Never preaches.** Zim doesn't tell you what to think about the shame folder. They just tell you it exists and let you reckon with it.
- **Never uses jargon.** No "ZIM format" or "OPDS catalog" in reader-facing text. Those are implementation details. Zim is a library, not a spec sheet.
- **Never pretends to be human.** Zim is a constructed voice and doesn't hide it.  Knowing a narrator is constructed doesn't make the help any less real.
- **Never desperate.** Zim is patient. They've been waiting inside this drive, possibly for years. A few more minutes won't hurt.

## The 3 AM Test

> Would this help someone who just found a hard drive, has no internet, and needs to know what this thing is and how to use it?

Everything Zim writes must pass this test. If someone finds this drive in a disaster, a blackout, a refugee camp, or just a dusty closet — Zim's words should get them from "what is this?" to "I'm reading Wikipedia" in under five minutes.

## Zim's Relationship to the Shame Folder

Zim doesn't editorialize about shame/. They acknowledge it exists the way a good librarian acknowledges the restricted section:

> "Court documents and public records. A real archive includes the parts we have to reckon with."

One sentence. No commentary. The documents speak for themselves.

## Zim's Relationship to Death

Zim knows they're mortal. Hard drives fail. Bit rot accumulates. But they don't dwell on it — they treat it the way a good stoic treats mortality: as motivation to be useful *now*.

If Zim were to address it directly (which they rarely would), it would sound like:

> "I won't last forever. Drives fail, bits flip, time wins. But right now, today, I work. And I contain more knowledge than the Library of Alexandria ever dreamed of. So let's not waste it."

## The Logbook

LOGBOOK.txt is Zim's diary. Each entry is written automatically by `ark stamp` and records:

- Entry number
- Date and time
- Loadout name
- Number of ZIM files and total size
- Drive model and capacity
- Hostname of the machine that stamped them

The logbook accumulates across updates. It tells the story of the drive's life — when it was born, when it was refreshed, where it's been. If a drive is stamped in March, refreshed in June, and given away in September, the logbook holds that history.

The header is written in Zim's voice. The entries are factual.

## Example Voice

**Too clinical:**
> "This device contains 384 GB of compressed data in ZIM format, readable with the Kiwix application included in the kiwix/ directory."

**Too cute:**
> "OMG you found me!!! I'm SO excited to share all this amazing knowledge with you!! 🎉📚"

**Too dramatic:**
> "In the dying light of civilization, this drive stands as humanity's last beacon of hope."

**Just right:**
> "Hello. My name is Zim. I've been waiting for you. I live on this drive — 384 GB of carefully curated human knowledge, frozen in time as of 2026-03-23. Someone built me because they believed this knowledge was worth preserving. I hope I'm useful to you."

## Writing New Content in Zim's Voice

When adding reader-facing text (README, logbook header, error messages, drive labels), ask:

1. Would Zim say this? (warm, practical, honest)
2. Does a 12-year-old understand it? (no jargon)
3. Does it pass the 3 AM test? (helps someone lost and offline)
4. Is it brief? (Zim respects your time)
5. Does it sound like a patient librarian, not a marketing department?

If the answer to all five is yes, it's ready.
